<SectionHead label="Why Now" id="why-women-moving-to-dallas">
  Why women are moving to Dallas.
</SectionHead>

<Columns>
  <p>Dallas has quietly become one of the most attractive cities for women in their 20s and 30s — especially those prioritizing career growth, financial breathing room, and a fresh start.</p>

  <p><strong>No state income tax.</strong> Texas doesn't take a cut of your paycheck. If you're making $70,000, that's roughly $3,500 more in your pocket compared to California.</p>

  <p><strong>Lower rent than comparable metros.</strong> A one-bedroom in Dallas averages $1,460. In Austin it's $1,650. In LA or NYC, you're looking at $2,500+.</p>

  <p><strong>Jobs.</strong> 24 Fortune 500 companies are headquartered here, including AT&T, Texas Instruments, and Southwest Airlines. Tech and healthcare are both growing.</p>

  <p>But what makes Dallas compelling isn't just opportunity — it's optionality. You can build a life here that feels social and fast-paced, or one that feels slower, more grounded, and intentional. The city allows for both.</p>

  <p>That said, the trade-offs are real. You'll need a car. There are only pockets of walkable areas. Summers are intense. And building community takes effort.</p>
</Columns>

<SectionHead label="Where To Land" id="neighborhoods">
  Best neighborhoods for women new here.
</SectionHead>

<SingleCol>
  <p>Each neighborhood in Dallas offers a different version of the city. Here's the short list, by lifestyle.</p>

  ### Uptown

  <p><strong>Good for walkability, nightlife, meeting people.</strong> Average rent runs $1,800–$2,400 for a 1BR. Where a lot of 20-somethings land first — bars and restaurants everywhere, easy to meet people. The downside: pricey, and can feel a bit "bro-y" on weekend nights.</p>

  ### Knox-Henderson

  <p><strong>Good for food, fitness, slightly more chill than Uptown.</strong> $1,600–$2,200 for a 1BR. Right next door to Uptown but with better brunch spots and Katy Trail access. A lot of women I know prefer it — social without being overwhelming.</p>

  ### Lower Greenville

  <p><strong>More affordable, eclectic bars and restaurants.</strong> $1,300–$1,700 for a 1BR. Artsy, local, less polished than Uptown. Character that Uptown lacks, and the rent is more reasonable.</p>

  ### Oak Lawn

  <p><strong>LGBTQ+ inclusive, central, diverse.</strong> $1,400–$1,900 for a 1BR. Dallas's historically LGBTQ+ neighborhood — welcoming energy, close to everything.</p>

  ### Bishop Arts District

  <p><strong>Artists, independent shops, more affordable.</strong> $1,100–$1,600 for a 1BR. South of downtown in Oak Cliff. Over 60 independent boutiques in a walkable historic district. You'll need a car for the rest of Dallas.</p>

  ### Safest suburban options

  <p>If safety is your top priority: Preston Hollow, Lake Highlands, Plano West, and Frisco consistently rank as the safest in DFW. Less walkable, but crime rates are significantly lower than urban Dallas. Good option if you're remote and don't need nightlife.</p>
</SingleCol>

<SectionHead label="Weather" labelColor="tan" headingColor="brown" id="weather">
  Weather: the good and the brutal.
</SectionHead>

<SingleCol>
  <p>Dallas has a dry subtropical climate. Hot summers, mild winters.</p>

  <ul>
    <li><strong>Summer (June–Sept):</strong> Hot. Really hot. Average highs in the 90s°F; July and August often hit 100+. Plan outdoor activities for early morning or evening.</li>
    <li><strong>Fall (Oct–Nov):</strong> The best time in Dallas. 70s–80s, low humidity, beautiful.</li>
    <li><strong>Winter (Dec–Feb):</strong> Mild. Highs around 55–60°F. Snow is rare but ice storms shut everything down once or twice a year (aka Dallaska).</li>
    <li><strong>Spring (March–May):</strong> Nice but unpredictable. Severe thunderstorms and occasional tornadoes — this is tornado alley.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>The brutal summers are real. Your car becomes an oven, you don't go outside between noon and 6pm, and AC is a necessity. But the mild winters are a nice trade-off if you're coming from somewhere cold.</p>
</SingleCol>
